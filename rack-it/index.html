            About Zargo

            Zargo is Rack It's own rating. Everyone starts at 500.

            It's odds on a rack. When two players are 100 apart, the higher one is expected to win two racks for every one the other wins. 200 apart, four for every one. Level ratings, one for one.

            A rated match moves both ratings once the other player confirms it on their own phone. A friendly uses the ratings for the handicap and moves nothing. Rated is a tick at setup, offered when both players have an account.

            Every rated match moves both ratings by how the racks went against those odds. Winning when you were expected to moves you a little; an upset moves you more. In 11-Point-Nine a rack counts by the share of its points you took. In every other game, 8-ball and 9-ball alike, it counts by who won the rack, at half the weight — points, fouls and balls left on the table never enter it. One match moves a settled rating by 40 at most.

            A handicap never changes what you earn. Level, split targets or a head start: the rating learns from the racks as they happened, and the handicap only makes the match close.

            Robustness is how many racks sit behind a rating. Under 30 it's provisional: still used for handicaps, but it moves quickly. A new player's first three matches against someone established move them most of the way to where their results put them.

            It isn't Fargo. Zargo borrows FargoRate's scale and its idea of robustness, but a Zargo of 500 and a FargoRate of 500 are unrelated numbers, and Cuescore's rating is different again.
